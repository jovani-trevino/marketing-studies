# Editorial checklist for marketing articles

Use this checklist to review analyses, guides, case studies and explanatory articles before publication. Tick an item after completing the check; record unresolved points at the end.

## 1. Question and contribution

- [ ] The article answers a specific question and identifies whom it can help.
- [ ] The summary explains what the reader will learn and the scope of the analysis.
- [ ] The title describes the content without promising results the article does not demonstrate.
- [ ] The original contribution is identifiable: analysis, documented experience, comparison, method or example.

## 2. Sources and attribution

- [ ] Figures, quotations and verifiable claims have an identifiable source near the relevant passage.
- [ ] I opened the sources and checked that they support the exact claim, period and context.
- [ ] I prioritise original research, official documentation and first-hand data when available.
- [ ] Each reference retains its author or institution, title, date and real URL or DOI. I have not added identifiers or references I could not verify.
- [ ] I distinguish what the source documents from my interpretation and personal recommendations.
- [ ] Direct quotations are accurate and brief; reused images, data and materials have the appropriate permission or attribution.

## 3. Method, data and limitations

- [ ] I explain where the data comes from and how it was selected, cleaned or compared.
- [ ] Metrics specify the population, period, unit and, for rates, numerator and denominator.
- [ ] Simulated examples are labelled illustrative and distinguished from actual observations.
- [ ] Calculations can be repeated using the information provided; I checked totals, percentages, dates and units.
- [ ] I describe limitations, missing data and alternative explanations. The conclusion stays within what the method supports.
- [ ] If I mention external review, peer review, a certification or a client result, I can substantiate the process or claim and have permission to publish it.

## 4. Editing and formatting

- [ ] The introduction frames the problem and the conclusion answers the initial question, with practical steps where appropriate.
- [ ] I define acronyms and technical terms on first use and remove repetition and ambiguous wording.
- [ ] Code, equations, tables and lists retain symbols, line breaks and units after being saved in the editor.
- [ ] ES/EN versions preserve the meaning, links, figures and limitations of the same article.

## 5. Accessible reading

- [ ] Headings describe each section and follow a coherent hierarchy.
- [ ] Links describe their destination; downloads identify the file type.
- [ ] Informative images have useful alternative text; decorative images do not add reading noise.
- [ ] Audiovisual content includes captions or transcripts as appropriate.
- [ ] I checked the mobile view, enlarged text, contrast and keyboard navigation. Animations allow comfortable reading and use of controls.

Refer to [W3C WAI guidance on writing accessible content](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/) when completing these checks.

## 6. Publication and maintenance

- [ ] Authorship and the publication date are accurate. An updated date reflects an actual revision.
- [ ] The cover, summary, category and search description represent the published article.
- [ ] I tested internal and external links, the language switcher and downloadable files.
- [ ] Screenshots, examples and files do not expose private information or credentials.
- [ ] I recorded the changes, unresolved points and claims that will require another review.

[Google Search Central guidance on helpful and reliable content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) provides additional questions about usefulness, clear authorship and original contribution.
